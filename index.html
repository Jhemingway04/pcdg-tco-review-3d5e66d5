The Case for Change

Why this is back at the top of the list.

The product fit was never the question — the last evaluation stalled only because the urgency cooled. The pressures that started this are all still true, and with the summer season winding down, leadership has asked to pick the POS decision back up now. Three forces are driving it regardless of how the ownership transition lands.

01

TeamWork forced an enterprise upgrade

Costs jumped from the ~$1,200–$1,500/mo you used to pay to $3,000–$3,500/mo — an enterprise-grade system that's overkill for a single store, at more than double the price.

02

Support has gone MIA

Service on the current platform has effectively disappeared. For a store running its highest-volume days on that system, that's an operational risk, not just an inconvenience.

03

~30 hours a week of manual entry

Receiving, tagging, and order management run on manual data entry today. That's roughly 30 hours every week of skilled staff time spent keeping the back office glued together.

The business is mid-transition to a strategic partner, which calls for flexibility, not a long lock-in. Shopify can be contracted for a one-year term now — so you're prepared to move day one — with the option to capture better pricing on a longer term once ownership settles.

The Bottom Line

Roughly the same monthly all-in — with real savings, hours back, and no re-platform ahead.

Against your ~$14M revenue band, Shopify lands at about the same monthly all-in spend you're at today, but with roughly $20,000 a year in real savings once the hidden TeamWork costs are removed — plus ~30 hours a week of manual entry eliminated and Shopify Payments at 2.3% + 10¢. The platform fee alone drops from an enterprise tier to a single-store plan, and because Shopify scales with you, this is a decision you don't have to revisit as the business grows.

1-year vs 3-year, side by side.
